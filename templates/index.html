        Eye Disease Information

        Educational overview of the four categories used by this model.

        Cataract

        A condition involving clouding of the eye's natural lens, which can affect vision.

        Possible symptoms: Blurry vision, glare, faded colours.

        Diabetic Retinopathy

        Diabetes-related damage to blood vessels in the retina.

        Possible symptoms: Blurred vision, floaters, changing vision.

        Glaucoma

        A group of eye conditions that can damage the optic nerve.

        Possible symptoms: Often no early symptoms; later vision loss may occur.

        Normal

        Important: This does not guarantee that the eye is disease-free.

        Laser Eye Surgery Guide

        Explore six laser vision-correction techniques, understand how these procedures work and learn about recovery and aftercare.

        Types of Laser Eye Surgery

        Learn about six techniques used for laser vision correction.

        1. LASIK

        LASIK creates a thin flap in the cornea. A laser reshapes the underlying corneal tissue to correct certain refractive errors.

        Key Points

        • Uses a corneal flap.
        • Vision often improves quickly.
        • May cause temporary dry eyes.
        • Corneal thickness must be assessed.
        Flap-based procedure

        2. PRK

        PRK removes the thin outer surface layer of the cornea. A laser then reshapes the cornea to correct vision.

        Key Points

        • Does not create a LASIK-style flap.
        • Surface layer heals naturally.
        • Initial recovery is slower than LASIK.
        • May be considered when LASIK is unsuitable.
        Surface laser procedure

        3. LASEK

        LASEK loosens the thin outer corneal layer, moves it aside and uses a laser to reshape the cornea before repositioning the layer.

        Key Points

        • Works on the corneal surface.
        • Does not use a LASIK-style flap.
        • Recovery takes time as the surface heals.
        • Temporary discomfort may occur.
        Surface-based technique

        4. Epi-LASIK

        Epi-LASIK uses a special instrument to separate the thin epithelial layer from the cornea. Laser treatment then reshapes the cornea.

        Key Points

        • Uses an epithelial separation technique.
        • Does not create a LASIK-style flap.
        • Surface healing is required.
        • Availability varies between clinics.
        Surface-based technique

        5. TransPRK

        TransPRK uses an excimer laser to remove the surface epithelial layer and reshape the cornea without using a surgical instrument for removal.

        Key Points

        • All-laser surface procedure.
        • No LASIK-style flap is created.
        • Surface healing is necessary.
        • Early recovery may involve discomfort.
        No-touch surface procedure

        6. SMILE

        SMILE uses a femtosecond laser to create a small piece of tissue inside the cornea. This tissue is removed through a small incision.

        Key Points

        • Uses a small incision.
        • Does not create a LASIK-style flap.
        • Can correct certain refractive errors.
        • Not suitable for every prescription.
        Small-incision procedure

        How Does Laser Eye Surgery Work?

        Understand the general process before and during laser vision-correction surgery.

        Before Surgery: Eye Assessment

        Eye Examination

        The specialist checks overall eye health and examines the patient's vision.

        Corneal Measurements

        Corneal thickness, shape and other measurements are evaluated.

        Prescription Check

        The specialist reviews the glasses prescription and checks its stability.

        General Surgery Steps

        The exact steps depend on the selected procedure.

        Step 1: Preparation

        The eye is examined, prepared and numbed using prescribed anaesthetic eye drops.

        Step 2: Laser Treatment

        The surgeon uses the appropriate laser technique to reshape the cornea.

        Step 3: Aftercare

        The specialist provides eye drops, protective instructions and follow-up appointments.

        Not every procedure follows the same steps. A qualified refractive surgeon decides which procedure is appropriate after an examination.

        Surgery Recovery Guide

        General recovery information and precautions after laser vision-correction procedures.

        Rest Your Eyes

        Rest after the procedure and avoid rubbing or pressing your eyes.

        Use Prescribed Drops

        Use prescribed eye drops exactly as directed by your eye specialist.

        Protect Your Eyes

        Follow advice about sunglasses, sunlight, dust and environmental exposure.

        Screen Time

        Follow your surgeon's advice about returning to screens and normal daily activities.

        Follow-up Visits

        Attend follow-up appointments so the specialist can monitor healing.

        Warning Signs

        Seek urgent medical advice for severe pain, sudden vision loss, increasing redness or rapidly worsening symptoms.

        Important Medical Disclaimer

        This module provides general educational information only. It does not diagnose eye conditions, recommend surgery or determine patient suitability. Laser vision correction has risks and may not eliminate the need for glasses. Only a qualified eye surgeon can assess individual suitability.
